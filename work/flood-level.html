Behind the experiment

Real terrain. A simplified question.

Free USDA NAIP aerial photography is draped over an elevation grid. OpenStreetMap supplies road and bridge alignments. Simple houses and road/bridge models are limited to the inner 0.33 miles. The outer area is aerial imagery draped over terrain, with no added models. Generated trees have been removed. House positions, dimensions and heights are approximate. The scene uses true vertical scale. The center marker and one-mile boundary are annotations.

What this version can tell you

How a chosen horizontal elevation compares with sampled ground. It cannot tell you how many feet the actual river must rise to flood your house. That requires an observed river reference, compatible vertical datums, surveyed building elevations, and hydraulic modeling.

Known limits

No river connectivity, flow, drainage, culverts, levees, building-interior flooding, rainfall, or flood timing. The scenery does not affect the water calculation. Terrain is sampled about every 20 meters; source resolution, age, and vertical accuracy vary. The circular mesh boundary is approximate. Do not use this for safety, insurance, or evacuation decisions.
